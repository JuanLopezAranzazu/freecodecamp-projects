Alan Turing

The mathematician who helped shape modern computing

A pioneer of computing

Alan Turing was a British mathematician, logician and computer scientist whose ideas fundamentally influenced the development of modern computing.

In 1936, Turing introduced the concept of the Turing machine, a mathematical model that helped define what it means for a problem to be computable.

During World War II, Turing worked at Bletchley Park as part of the British effort to break encrypted communications. His work on German ciphers contributed significantly to Allied intelligence.

Turing also made important contributions to artificial intelligence. His famous question, "Can machines think?", inspired what became known as the Turing Test.

Important moments

  • 1912 — Alan Turing is born in London, England.
  • 1936 — Publishes his influential work on computable numbers and introduces the Turing machine.
  • 1939–1945 — Works at Bletchley Park on cryptographic analysis during World War II.
  • 1950 — Publishes his influential paper "Computing Machinery and Intelligence".
  • 1954 — Dies at the age of 41.
